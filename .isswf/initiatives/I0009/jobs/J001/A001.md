## POSIX analysis (reported, not changed)
- On POSIX `child.kill()` sends SIGTERM to the direct child only; the child is not `detached`, so there is no process group to signal.
- A wrapper that forks instead of exec'ing, or any child whose descendants inherit stdout/stderr, would keep the pipes open and `close` would wait: the same hang. A child that ignores SIGTERM also hangs (no SIGKILL escalation).
- The 2 s bounded settle is win32-only, so acceptance 2's bound does not hold on POSIX. That follows "POSIX unchanged".
- Practical exposure is low: callers spawn real executables directly (pi's POSIX bin is a shebang script, not a shell wrapper).
- Possible follow-up: `detached: true` plus `process.kill(-pid, ...)` with SIGKILL escalation and a bounded settle.
